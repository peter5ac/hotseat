# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Kids (ages 7-14) playing together in social contexts — sleepovers, FaceTime calls, family game nights. The front camera shows their face as part of the experience, creating a "hot seat" dynamic where friends watch each other make tough ranking choices.

## Product Purpose

A social ranking game where players see items from fun categories one at a time and tap to assign them to their top-5 slots. The "blind" mechanic (you don't know what's coming next) forces gut-instinct choices that spark debate and laughter. Success looks like kids grabbing the iPad/phone and passing it around without needing instructions.

## Brand Personality

Chill, expressive, personal. This is about taste and self-expression, not competition or hyperactivity. The vibe is "cool kid showing you their favorites" — confident but not loud, opinionated but not aggressive.

## Anti-references

- **Generic quiz apps** (Kahoot, Buzzfeed quizzes): No loud gradients, countdown timers, leaderboard anxiety, or stock illustrations.
- **Educational/EdTech**: Must never feel like homework, a learning tool, or something a teacher assigned.
- **Social media feeds**: No likes, followers, content feeds, or engagement mechanics. This is a moment, not a platform.

## Design Principles

- **Grab and go**: Zero onboarding, zero accounts. Pick a category and play.
- **The face is the content**: The camera background isn't decorative — it's the social layer. UI exists over and around the player's face.
- **Two kinds of play.** *Expressive games* (Hot Take) have no scores, winners, or timers — they're about revealing taste, not winning. *Skill games* (Speed Tap, Lane Dodge) may have scores, lives, and fail states, but stay per-session only: no leaderboards, no persistent ranks, no accounts. Either way: nothing is recorded, uploaded, or shared.
- **Pass-the-phone ready**: Every state must be legible at arm's length and tappable by imprecise fingers.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Minimal formal requirements. Priorities: large tap targets (kids + excitement = imprecise taps), sufficient contrast over a live camera feed (the dimming overlay is load-bearing), and readability at the font sizes used.
